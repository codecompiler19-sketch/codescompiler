# What Is SEO?

Search engine optimization (SEO) is the work of helping search engines understand a website and helping people find its pages when they search for relevant information, products, or services. SEO focuses on organic results rather than advertisements that are paid for by the click or impression.

For example, someone searching for “how to compress images for a website” probably wants practical instructions. A useful page should explain the options, demonstrate a method, and discuss the trade-offs—not simply repeat that phrase in every paragraph.

## How organic search works

Search engines discover pages by following links and other known URLs, crawl accessible content, and process it for possible inclusion in their index. When a person searches, systems evaluate many signals to decide which results are relevant. The exact systems are complex and change over time, so SEO should focus on helping users rather than trying to reverse-engineer a guaranteed ranking formula.

Being crawled does not guarantee indexing, and indexing does not guarantee a high position.

## Main areas of SEO

### Content and search intent

Understand what a reader needs and provide a clear, accurate answer. A page targeting “best laptop for programming students” should explain relevant considerations and comparisons, while a page targeting “what is a variable in Python” should define the concept and show code.

### On-page SEO

Use a clear page title, a descriptive main heading, logically nested subheadings, useful internal links, and meaningful image alt text where an image conveys information. See [on-page SEO techniques](/tutorial/seo-on-page-techniques/).

### Technical SEO

Make important pages accessible to crawlers and people. Check status codes, mobile layout, performance, canonical URLs, internal navigation, and sitemap configuration when appropriate.

### Off-page SEO

Earn attention and relevant links through useful resources, original research, community participation, and relationships. Learn more in [off-page SEO techniques](/tutorial/seo-off-page-techniques/).

## SEO versus paid search

SEO can attract visits without paying for each organic click, but it requires ongoing work and offers no fixed timetable or ranking guarantee. Paid search can provide visibility through advertising budgets, but traffic may stop when campaigns stop. Many businesses use both for different goals.

## A simple example

A local plumber publishes a page explaining emergency pipe repairs, the areas served, the typical process, and how customers can contact the business. The page uses a descriptive title and works well on mobile. These choices help visitors understand the service and give search engines clearer context. They do not guarantee a top ranking; relevance, competition, location, and many other factors can affect results.

## What SEO is not

SEO is not repeating a keyword a set number of times, inserting keywords into hidden metadata, buying large quantities of links, or promising a first-place result. Search engines can choose different title links or snippets from page content, and a meta description is not a guaranteed ranking boost.

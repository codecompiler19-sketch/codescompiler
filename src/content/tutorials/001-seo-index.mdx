# SEO Tutorial for Beginners

Search engine optimization (SEO) is the practice of making a website easier for people to discover through unpaid search results. It combines useful content, clear page structure, accessible technical foundations, and a reputation that earns attention from other websites. SEO can help the right readers find a page, but no technique can guarantee a particular ranking.

Imagine you run a small bakery in Delhi. A page explaining your birthday cakes, showing original photos, listing delivery areas, and answering common questions gives customers and search engines more useful information than a page that repeats “best bakery” dozens of times.

## How search engines find pages

Search engines generally work through three stages:

- **Crawling:** automated systems discover URLs and fetch page content.
- **Indexing:** systems process the content and decide whether it is suitable to store in the search index.
- **Serving results:** when someone searches, the engine selects and orders results it considers relevant and useful.

A page can be technically accessible without being indexed, and an indexed page is not guaranteed to rank prominently.

## The main parts of SEO

- **Content and search intent:** answer the question the visitor came to solve.
- **On-page SEO:** use descriptive titles, useful headings, meaningful links, and relevant image descriptions.
- **Technical SEO:** help crawlers access important pages; maintain a logical URL structure, mobile usability, and reliable performance.
- **Off-page SEO:** earn genuine mentions and links by publishing work people find worth referencing.
- **Measurement:** use search and analytics data to understand impressions, clicks, engagement, and meaningful outcomes.

## A practical first-page checklist

Suppose you publish a tutorial about making a responsive navigation bar. Give it a specific title, explain the result near the beginning, include working HTML and CSS, show the expected behaviour, and link to related lessons such as [on-page SEO techniques](/tutorial/seo-on-page-techniques/) only where the link genuinely helps the reader. Make sure the page works on a phone and that its main content is available to visitors and crawlers.

Avoid keyword stuffing, copied articles, hidden text, and paid link schemes intended to manipulate rankings. These approaches can damage trust and may violate search engine spam policies.

## How to evaluate progress

Record a baseline before changing a page. In a search performance tool, review impressions, clicks, click-through rate, and queries that lead people to the page. Also check whether visitors complete the task the page was designed to support. Results may take time and can vary; use the evidence to decide what to improve next.
